Rethinking the library for phones

The desktop aisle is a 3D walk. On a phone it's a narrow corridor you can barely look down, and the initiatives act already owns the bookshelf. Three phone-first alternatives below, all interactive, all using the real stories. Each keeps the library's materials (walnut, brass, paper, crimson) so desktop and phone still feel like the same place.

Concept A

The Card Catalogue recommended

A library card-catalogue cabinet. Each drawer is a cohort year; tap a drawer and it slides out, index cards rise out of the tray, and you swipe through them. Every story is a typed index card with its accession number and a "Read →". The last card in the newest drawer is the reserved blank card: "Your story belongs here."

Tap the drawers · swipe the cards

Why this one

  • Unmistakably a library object, but not a shelf, so it sits next to the initiatives bookcase without repeating it.
  • Titles are horizontal and full-length on the cards; nothing truncates.
  • Scales: one drawer per cohort year (same grouping as the desktop aisle's planAisle), cards swipe inside. Today that's a full 2026 drawer and an empty 2027 one waiting for the next founder.
  • Motion is small and physical: drawer slide (translate), cards rise in with a stagger. Reduced motion: drawers open instantly.
  • Reuses the walnut and brass tokens from the phone bookcase, so both acts read as the same room.

Build notes

  • New StoriesCatalogue, rendered by ShelfAct below 769px instead of StoriesAisle; the aisle stops pinning on phones again.
  • Newest drawer open by default; "Read →" opens the existing StoryReader overlay (already in StoriesAisle.tsx).
  • Curtain: back to the short phone fade-up like the other acts.
Concept B

The Reading-Room Stack

Books lying flat in one tall stack under a reading lamp, with horizontal spines you can actually read. The section pins and your scroll moves through the stack: the book at the lamp slides half out and its checkout slip shows the story. It's the closest match to desktop, where scrolling also "walks".

Scroll inside the phone

Trade-offs

  • Keeps the desktop's scroll-driven feel with a pinned runway, but on a much simpler scene (no 3D corridor).
  • Readable spines; the slip gives the excerpt without opening anything.
  • Pinned sections on phones fight the iOS URL bar and quick flicks; the stack needs about 1.5 screens of runway per 4 stories, so it grows long with 30 stories.
  • A stack of books sits close to the initiatives bookcase visually.
Concept C

Front Pages

Each story as a full-bleed front page you tap through, like stories on Instagram: progress ticks on top, tap the right side for next and the left for back, and it autoplays every 5 seconds. The last page is the invitation. Fastest to scan, and the most familiar gesture on a phone.
